# Phase 2 source data — national Direkter Verkehr tariff

Generated by `cargo run -p isodapane-build -- fetch-national build-national`.
Do not edit by hand; re-run the pipeline instead.

## Where this came from, and why not T601

The spec planned to transcribe the **T601** price table from a PDF and warned
that tariff kilometres (Tarifkilometer, Distanzzuschläge) "are not published
machine-readably". Two corrections:

1. **The tariff PDFs are login-gated.** T601 (`download=16350`), T603 and T604
   on `allianceswisspass.ch` all return the Alliance SwissPass *login page*, not
   the document, for every URL form tried. Transcribing T601 is not possible
   from open sources.

2. **It is not needed.** opentransportdata.swiss publishes the
   [OSDM Offline File](https://data.opentransportdata.swiss/en/dataset/osdm-offline)
   — "Subset of fares in Swiss public transport in OSDM format" — which carries,
   per relation, the **tariff distance**, the **permitted via-route**, and the
   **actual price** by passenger type, class and reduction card. That is the
   price table and the tariff kilometres together, authoritative and machine
   readable.

Source file: `osdm_delivery_10_7.json` (105 MB) inside `osdm_delivery_10_7.zip`,
delivery 10.7, OSDM version 1.4, `usage: PRODUCTION`, carrier SBB.
**Published 2023-03-30**, so prices are 2023-era and below current fares; re-fetch
before shipping any number to users.

Terms: opentransportdata.swiss open data terms. The portal states plainly that
"the existing data do not constitute a sufficient condition for ticket sales by
third parties" — this is for modelling, not selling.

## Files

| File | Rows | What |
|---|---|---|
| `stations.json` | 508 | UIC code → station name. This is a **subset** of the network, not all of Switzerland. |
| `relations.json` | 8332 | One permitted route between two stations: `km` (tariff distance), `route` (via UIC codes), and prices `adult2`, `adult1`, `half2`, `half1`, `child2` in CHF. |
| `distance_price.json` | 561 | Empirical price-per-tariff-kilometre curve: `n`, `min`, `median`, `max` for 2nd and 1st class at each distance. |

A city pair appears **once per permitted route**, each with its own distance and
price — e.g. Zürich HB → Bern is 164 km / CHF 51.00 via Olten and 194 km /
CHF 59.00 via Luzern. Pricing a journey means pricing the route actually taken,
not the pair.

Halbtax and child columns come out at half the adult fare, as the tariff
prescribes, and A→B matches B→A throughout.

## The finding that matters for Phase 2

**Tariff distance does not determine price.** Measured over the 8330 priced
relations:

- 480 of 561 distinct distances carry **more than one price**
- 8070 relations (**96.9%**) sit at such an ambiguous distance
- spread at a single distance: **median 12.9%, p90 47.1%, max 273.3%**
  (CHF 46.60 between the cheapest and dearest relation of equal length)

For example 229 km costs CHF 65.00 (Luzern–Lausanne) but 230 km costs CHF 60.40
(Basel–Lausanne). Those are the Distanzzuschläge and operator-specific kilometre
rates the spec anticipated, and they are large.

So a Phase 2 map priced by `distance → T601 band` alone would be **wrong by a
median of ~13% and occasionally by far more**. This dataset is what makes that
measurable: it is both the calibration set and the honest error bar. Any national
map built on distance must publish this distribution rather than claim precision
— and where a relation exists here, its real price should be preferred over any
estimate.

## Regional fare unions — `fare_unions.json`

From SBB's [Tarifverbundkarte](https://data.sbb.ch/explore/dataset/tarifverbundkarte/)
(`fetch-fare-unions`, `build-fare-unions`). Unlike the OSDM snapshot this is
maintained continuously — the copy used here was modified the same day it was
fetched.

**20 fare unions covering all 2137 Swiss municipalities**: Libero (398+45),
Frimobil (341+157), A-Welle (231), OSTWIND (225+3), ZVV (175), TNW (149),
Arcobaleno (116), Passepartout (115+23), Vagabond (61), Léman Pass (45),
unireso (45), TV Schwyz (21), Engadin Mobil (13), Transreno (10), and the
single-municipality unions Davos and Klosters.

**This is coverage, not prices.** It answers "which regional tariff applies
here", which is the prerequisite for widening beyond the rail tariff — but none
of the twenty publishes a machine-readable zone plan or price table. The Zürich
half of this project needed a cantonal WFS for zone polygons plus a
hand-transcribed price table; pricing all twenty means repeating that twenty
times, against twenty different publication practices. `fare_unions.json` carries
each union's code, name, colour, municipality count and tariff URL as the
starting point.
