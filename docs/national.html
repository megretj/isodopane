What this is, and what it isn't

The national tariff charges by tariff kilometres, not zones, so this map is a distance model rather than the zone model used for Zürich. Routing follows the fastest train, not the cheapest detour, and the fare is computed on the line actually taken. Every station is priced one of two ways, and the hover tells you which.

  • Published fare — a real price exists for that station pair in the open fare data, and it is used as-is. 2585 pairs.
  • Estimate — no published price, so tariff kilometres are summed along the route and looked up in the fare curve. Measured against held-out real fares: median error 6.7%, nine in ten within 19%.
  • A nearer station may cost more than a farther one, and that is not an error. A published fare is a direct relation between two stations, priced for the journey you would actually make between them. From Zürich HB, Liestal is CHF 38.00 while Basel SBB further down the same line is CHF 31.60 — because the fast Basel train runs through Liestal without stopping, so reaching Liestal means a slower line or a longer way round. Both fares are right; forcing them into order would misprice one.
  • Switzerland only. Foreign stations are excluded, so a journey is never routed through Italy or Germany even where a real train would. A handful of border lines are cut off as a result.
  • Tariff kilometres are not map kilometres. They are fitted per stretch of line from real fares, because surcharges are large and uneven: Wengen–Lauterbrunnen is 1.2 km on the ground and 16 tariff km on the ticket. Lines no published fare covers keep their geographic length and will be underpriced if they carry a surcharge.
  • Fare data is from 2023 and sits below today's prices. Treat every number as indicative.
  • Rail only, and only stations a train calls at. No buses, no postbus, no city networks, no regional fare unions.
  • Single full-fare tickets. No GA, day passes, saver tickets or supplements. Halbtax is taken as half the full fare.
  • No time dimension: this says what a place costs, not how long it takes.

Not a ticket shop, and not fit for selling travel. Check SBB before you buy.
