What this is, and what it isn't

Each zone is shaded by the cheapest single ticket that reaches any stop inside it, using the ZVV zone tariff. Zones 110 (Zürich) and 120 (Winterthur) each count as two, as the tariff requires. A ticket must cover every zone your vehicle passes through, so a non-stop express is charged for the zones it crosses without stopping.

  • Single tickets only. A GA travels free; a Halbtax pays the reduced column. Day passes and season tickets change the picture entirely.
  • No time dimension. This says what a place costs, not whether getting there takes 15 minutes or three hours on two buses.
  • Boundary stops. Some stops are tariff-valid in two or more zones. Each stop here is assigned exactly one zone, which overprices a few journeys through them.
  • Ends at the ZVV boundary. Journeys beyond it need Z-Pass or national fares, which are not modelled.
  • Scheduled network. Seasonal and very infrequent services count as connections regardless of whether you could realistically use them.
  • Route geometry is approximated. The feed carries no route shapes, so zones crossed between two stops are estimated from the straight line between them.
  • A few lake boat piers, the Polybahn, the Felsenegg cable car and the Meilen–Horgen car ferry appear as separate islands in the timetable data and are not reachable in this model.

Fares are computed from published zone rules, not bought from ZVV. Check the ZVV app before you travel.
